# What photos to ask for

Inspect what the user already supplied. Name the missing view and the feature it will resolve. There is no fixed shot count. A ring or spacer often needs just a face-on view (outer profile, teeth and opening) and a square side view (height and steps). For an asymmetric bracket, start with top, front and one end. Add the underside only if it has different geometry, or a close-up for a clip, countersink or obscured interface. An angled overview helps establish how faces join but is a poor measuring view.

For photos only, either show a known scale beside the part or include one known physical dimension in the same plane. If nothing supplies scale, identify the geometry first and ask for one reference before producing a sized STL. A coin or unknown sheet size is not a known scale.

Millimeter graph paper is ideal for flat profiles. Ask the user to lay the part on flat paper, leave grid visible around all sides, and confirm the spacing of the small squares (often 1 mm; bold lines can be 5 or 10 squares apart). If home-printed, check a multi-square span with a ruler. Hold the regular camera square to the sheet, center the part, keep both the edges and grid sharp and send the original, uncropped photo. For a thick part, put the grid alongside the face at the same height. For a side profile, stand the part on edge or place a vertical grid alongside it at the depth of the profile. Recalibrate each view. Avoid wide-angle shots and close-ups that distort the edges.

If the user prefers video, use a 30–60 second orbit in bright diffuse light, pausing on mating faces and flipping to show the underside. Original MOV or MP4 plus a known dimension or separate measurement photo works on Android or iPhone.

If calipers are needed, ask for the particular span and show where to put the jaws. Show contacts, reading and unit together, with the jaws zeroed first. For blind depth, use the depth rod; for a shaft flat, measure the flat to the opposite side. If the missing feature is broken, ask for the surviving fragment or mating part. JPEG, PNG and HEIC stills are supported.
